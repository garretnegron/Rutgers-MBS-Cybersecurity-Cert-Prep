# Terms and Definitions - Course 2

## A

**Assess**  
The fifth step of the **NIST RMF** that determines whether established controls are implemented correctly.

**Asset**  
An item perceived as having value to an organization.

**Attack Vectors**  
The pathways attackers use to penetrate security defenses.

**Authentication**  
The process of verifying who someone is.

**Authorization**  
The concept of granting access to specific resources in a system.

**Authorize**  
The sixth step of the **NIST RMF** that refers to being accountable for security and privacy risks within an organization.

**Availability**  
The principle that data is accessible to authorized users when needed.

---

## B

**Biometrics**  
Unique physical characteristics that can be used to verify a person's identity.

**Business Continuity**  
An organization's ability to maintain operations and productivity by establishing risk and disaster recovery plans.

---

## C

**Categorize**  
The second step of the **NIST RMF**, used to develop risk management processes and tasks.

**Chronicle**  
A cloud-native tool designed to retain, analyze, and search data.

**Confidentiality**  
The principle that only authorized users can access specific assets or data.

**CIA Triad (Confidentiality, Integrity, Availability)**  
A model that helps organizations evaluate and manage security risks when creating systems and policies.

---

## D

**Detect**  
A **NIST CSF** core function focused on identifying potential security incidents and improving monitoring capabilities.

---

## E

**Encryption**  
The process of converting readable data into an encoded format.

**External Threat**  
Anything outside an organization that has the potential to harm organizational assets.

---

## I

**Identify**  
A **NIST CSF** core function related to managing cybersecurity risk and its impact on an organization's people and assets.

**Implement**  
The fourth step of the **NIST RMF**, involving implementation of security and privacy controls.

**Incident Response**  
An organization's effort to identify an attack, contain damage, and recover from a security breach.

**Integrity**  
The principle that data is correct, authentic, and reliable.

**Internal Threat**  
A current or former employee, vendor, or trusted partner who poses a security risk.

---

## L

**Log**  
A record of events that occur within an organization's systems.

---

## M

**Metrics**  
Technical measurements such as response time, availability, and failure rate used to assess software performance.

**Monitor**  
The seventh step of the **NIST RMF**, which focuses on continuously observing system operations.

---

## N

**NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF)**  
A voluntary framework consisting of standards, guidelines, and best practices for managing cybersecurity risk.

**NIST Special Publication (SP) 800-53**  
A framework for protecting information systems within the U.S. federal government.

---

## O

**OWASP (Open Web Application Security Project)**  
A nonprofit organization dedicated to improving software security.

**Operating System (OS)**  
The interface between computer hardware and the user.

---

## P

**Playbook**  
A documented set of procedures that describes operational actions and responses.

**Prepare**  
The first step of the **NIST RMF**, involving activities necessary to manage security and privacy risks before incidents occur.

**Protect**  
A **NIST CSF** core function focused on implementing safeguards that mitigate cybersecurity threats.

---

## R

**Ransomware**  
A malicious attack in which threat actors encrypt data and demand payment for restoration.

**Recover**  
A **NIST CSF** core function focused on restoring systems and operations after an incident.

**Respond**  
A **NIST CSF** core function concerned with containing, analyzing, and improving responses to security incidents.

**Risk**  
Anything that can negatively impact the **Confidentiality**, **Integrity**, or **Availability (CIA)** of an asset.

**Risk Mitigation**  
The process of implementing controls and procedures to reduce the impact of risks.

---

## S

**Security Audit**  
A review of an organization's security controls, policies, and procedures against established standards.

**Security Controls**  
Safeguards designed to reduce specific security risks.

**Security Frameworks**  
Guidelines used to build plans that mitigate risks and threats to data and privacy.

**Security Information and Event Management (SIEM)**  
An application that collects and analyzes log data to monitor critical organizational activities.

**Security Orchestration, Automation, and Response (SOAR)**  
A collection of tools, applications, and workflows that automate responses to security events.

**Security Posture**  
An organization's ability to defend critical assets and respond to changing threats.

**Select**  
The third step of the **NIST RMF**, involving the selection, customization, and documentation of security controls.

**Shared Responsibility**  
The principle that everyone in an organization contributes to reducing risk and maintaining security.

**Social Engineering**  
A manipulation technique that exploits human behavior to gain unauthorized access, information, or valuables.

**Splunk Cloud**  
A cloud-hosted platform used to collect, search, and monitor log data.

**Splunk Enterprise**  
A self-hosted platform used to retain, analyze, and search log data while providing real-time security monitoring and alerts.

---

## T

**Threat**  
Any circumstance or event that can negatively impact organizational assets.

---

## V

**Vulnerability**  
A weakness that can be exploited by a threat.

---

# Key Concepts to Memorize

## NIST RMF Steps

1. **Prepare**
2. **Categorize**
3. **Select**
4. **Implement**
5. **Assess**
6. **Authorize**
7. **Monitor**

---

## NIST CSF Functions

- **Identify**
- **Protect**
- **Detect**
- **Respond**
- **Recover**

---

## CIA Triad

- **Confidentiality** → Only authorized users can access data
- **Integrity** → Data is accurate and trustworthy
- **Availability** → Data is accessible when needed

---

## Security Tools

- **SIEM** → Collects and analyzes logs
- **SOAR** → Automates security responses
- **Chronicle** → Cloud-native security analytics
- **Splunk Cloud** → Cloud-hosted log management
- **Splunk Enterprise** → Self-hosted log management

---

## Common Exam Favorites

- **Authentication** = Verify identity
- **Authorization** = Determine access
- **Risk** = Threat to CIA of an asset
- **Threat** = Potential danger
- **Vulnerability** = Weakness
- **Encryption** = Protect confidentiality
- **Incident Response** = Detect → Contain → Recover
- **Business Continuity** = Keep operating during disruptions
